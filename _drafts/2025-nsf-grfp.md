In the summer of 2025, I was awarded the NSF-GRFP. The NSF-GRFP is a fellowship for PhD students, and most PhD students are encouraged to apply. After using countless resources, and retroactively serving on a panel and sharing my materials with applicants, I decided to write a blog post with my advice & materials.

I had applied for the NSF-GRFP twice, once during the year before I started grad school, and once during my first year of grad school. During my first application cycle, I was in the middle of a gap year and not actively in touch with any professors, aside from emailing about recommendation letters. I didn't have anyone read my statements except for my best friend from college who was applying at the same time. Without too much guidance for the personal statement, and especially the research statement, I wasn't too surprised when I received my rejection. Nonetheless, I was able to get some helpful feedback by the time the NSF-GRFP reviews came around, so I revised my statements, wrote a completely new research proposal, and was awarded the GRFP on my second cycle.

Here, I share my research proposal, which most of my advice pertains to. Feel free to reach out if you want to see my earlier materials, reviews, or personal statement!

At the time I was preparing my application, I had little to know idea what I was doing (at least research-wise). In my first application, I wrote my research proposal about some research I had done in undergrad.

While doing my second application, I had just started a PhD program in Computer Science, after completing my B.S. in pure math. Much of my familiarity with my current field came from reading I did during Fall 2025 (at the same time I was applying for the NSF-GRFP). I had no clue how to write a proposal in this field, nor did I have any idea what was interesting or had already been done.

I am extremely grateful for my advisor, who helped come up with ideas on what to write my research proposal on. That being said, I think reviewers do care more about your ability to write a research proposal, rather than the nitty-gritty details about what research is being proposed. ### Anticipate and address challenges

As I eluded to before, at the time of my second application, I had no idea what I was doing. Believe it or not, this makes it pretty hard to write a research proposal. Even when I knew what I was writing about, I had no idea if an experiment I was proposing was realistic, if the timeline of my project was realistic at all, or if it even made sense. While these points should be refined when writing your statement, no one will be able to get this just right.

I think one of the things that makes a research proposal stronger is to anticipate and address challenges you might encounter: generally, if X happens, I'll re-evaluate Y and then do Z. Maybe your problem definition does not make sense, and you have to make more assumptions, or maybe you realize your experiment will take way too long, and you have to make some modification. Whatever it may be, it's helpful if you notice and point it out in your application.
